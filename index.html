What the numbers are

Estimates drawn from peer-reviewed publications, NASA and ESA mission data, expert consensus, biosignature detection confidence, and habitability assessments. A definitive claim requires 5-sigma confidence — about 99.99997% certainty.

Why they stay low

Multiple independent confirmations are required, and every abiotic explanation has to be ruled out first. Promising signals have repeatedly turned out to be non-biological chemistry. Every biosignature here remains inconclusive.

When it could resolve

A first definitive detection could come within 10 to 20 years — from returned Mars samples, from direct sampling of the Enceladus plume, or from what Europa Clipper finds beneath the ice.

Sources and coverage

Stories are collected every two hours by a harvester in WelcomeToYourGalaxy/space-life-news, reading institutional newsrooms, science desks, the arXiv preprint server and Google News regional editions in many languages across ten world regions. A story is kept only when it concerns the search for life — a launch, a telescope or a Mars landscape photograph does not qualify. Nothing is written or summarised here: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own and every row links to the original. A wire that returns zero is shown as zero, because coverage is uneven by language and the counts say where.

Compiled from NASA, ESA, Nature, The Astrophysical Journal and peer-reviewed astrobiology research. Probability figures are assessments of current evidence, not measurements, and carry no certainty.
