What the numbers are

Drawn from peer-reviewed work, NASA and ESA mission data, expert consensus and habitability assessments. A definitive claim requires 5-sigma confidence — about 99.99997% certainty.

Why they stay low

Independent confirmations are required and every abiotic explanation ruled out first. Promising signals have repeatedly proved to be non-biological chemistry. Every biosignature here is inconclusive.

When it could resolve

A first detection could come within 10 to 20 years — from returned Mars samples, direct sampling of the Enceladus plume, or what Europa Clipper finds beneath the ice.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Stories are collected every two hours by a harvester reading institutional newsrooms, science desks, the arXiv preprint server and Google News regional editions in many languages across ten world regions. A story is kept only when it concerns the search for life — a launch, a telescope or a Mars landscape photograph does not qualify. Nothing is written or summarised here: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own and every row links to the original. A wire that returns zero is shown as zero, because coverage is uneven by language and the counts say where.
